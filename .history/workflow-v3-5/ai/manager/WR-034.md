## Cohort and target principles
Primary scope: returning QB/RB/WR/TE consistent with the WR-033/WR-025 returner boundary.

At minimum model/score:
- target recorded games on a bounded NFL regular-season scale appropriate to the target season;
- zero-game players as valid availability outcomes;
- a low-availability event compatible with the accepted `<=8 recorded games` WR-027 warning label for comparison/calibration.

Additional events such as `>=14 games` or complete/near-complete availability may be evaluated if frozen before scoring and clearly justified.

Do not silently treat games played as a pure injury outcome. Games missed can arise from injury, suspension, role loss, benching, transactions, retirement, or other causes unless a source permits trustworthy decomposition.

## Transparent baselines
At least compare against a simple deterministic baseline using only preseason-known prior information, such as previous-season recorded games clamped to the valid target-season range.

R&D may define an additional training-only historical/position baseline if it is frozen before scoring and does not leak target-season information.

## Candidate architecture
Prefer transparent, calibrated candidates before opaque complexity. R&D may compare bounded regression/count/distributional or hurdle-style candidates when methodologically appropriate, but every candidate family and selection rule must be frozen before scoring.

A useful result may be:
- a direct expected-games model;
- a calibrated distribution/hurdle whose expectation is expected games;
- retention of a transparent baseline plus separate warning probabilities;
- or a negative result stating current evidence does not justify a learned expected-games model.

Do not force a model to win.

## Input/source policy
Start with rights-clean, cutoff-safe prior-season / immutable information already admitted by WR-025/WR-029 when relevant. Examples may include:
- previous-season and prior-two-season recorded games;
- age / experience;
- position;
- immutable draft metadata;
- accepted prior production/opportunity summaries only if they are frozen as candidate features before scoring.

Medical/injury, transaction, roster, depth, suspension, or availability-specific sources may enter only after explicit rights, point-in-time, historical coverage, revision, and maintainability review. Ambiguous sources must be excluded rather than reconstructed from hindsight.

Current mutable roster/status fields may not be used as historical target-preseason predictors without an as-of archive that passes PIT review.

## Required evaluation
Report pooled and by QB/RB/WR/TE, plus target-season stability where sample permits:

### Expected games
- MAE;
- RMSE;
- mean bias / calibration by predicted range;
- rank or correlation diagnostics only when meaningful and explicitly secondary.

### Availability events / probabilities
For `<=8 games` and any other predeclared event:
- prevalence;
- Brier score / Brier skill;
- ROC AUC and PR AUC when estimable;
- log loss where probabilities are valid;
- reliability/calibration bins;
- event rate in risk tiers/quantiles.

### Distribution / uncertainty
If a predictive distribution or interval is proposed:
- empirical interval coverage;
- sharpness/width;
- calibration by position and prediction range;
- failure cases / tail behavior.

### Robustness
- repeated-player-aware uncertainty for the primary baseline-vs-candidate comparison;
- season-to-season stability;
- coverage/missingness by position/season;
- source-omission and plausible perturbation sensitivity for selected optional inputs;
- fallback behavior when required/optional sources are unavailable.
